# Public LinkedIn profile review

Use when the user supplies a LinkedIn public ID or URL and expects a review without sharing an exported PDF.

## Procedure

1. Normalize an ID such as `example` to `https://www.linkedin.com/in/example`.
2. Open the exact URL and verify visible identity signals (name, current company, location, school, follower/connection counts). Do not assume the first search result is the right person.
3. Close login overlays when possible and inspect the public accessibility tree or visible page text.
4. Record only what is actually visible: headline/title, About preview, services, current company, certifications, recommendations, and activity topics.
5. LinkedIn may mask experience titles/dates or require authentication. State that limitation and request **LinkedIn → More/Resources → Save to PDF** or screenshots for a complete review.
6. Compare visible text with the user's current facts. High-value checks include:
   - stated years of experience versus actual tenure;
   - old implementation identity versus current architecture/leadership scope;
   - outdated services and keywords;
   - public company label versus legal employer/client assignment;
   - recent activity supporting or contradicting the desired positioning.
7. Deliver an initial review from public evidence, but do not call it a full résumé audit until the complete experience history is available.

## Common findings for senior technologists

- An About section often opens with an old stack and understates current architecture, business, leadership, or AI scope.
- Services may remain operational (testing/web development) after the person has moved into architecture or consulting.
- Strong recommendations can supply evidence of mentoring, influence, autonomy, and delivery impact; quote or paraphrase them accurately.
- Older certifications show depth but should not dominate the present-day value proposition.
- Recent activity on the target domain can reinforce repositioning, but likes alone are weaker than authored posts, case studies, talks, or featured artifacts.

## Safe employer/client wording

If the user is legally employed by Vendor and assigned to Client, draft one of:

- `Role — Vendor | assigned to Client`
- `Role | Vendor — consulting engagement at Client`
- `Vendor: Role (Client project)`

Do not list Client as direct employer unless the user confirms that relationship. If naming the client may be sensitive, use a sector description instead.
